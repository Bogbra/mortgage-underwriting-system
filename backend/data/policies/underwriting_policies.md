# Conventional Mortgage Underwriting Policy Manual

This manual defines the underwriting guidelines used by the specialist
agents. It is illustrative reference material for a portfolio project and
loosely mirrors typical conventional-loan (Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac style)
guidelines; it is not legal or compliance advice.

## 1. Credit Policy

### 1.1 Minimum Credit Score

The minimum credit score for a conventional loan is 620. Applicants below
620 do not meet conventional loan requirements and should be evaluated for
alternative loan products or declined. Scores of 740 and above qualify for
the best available pricing tier.

### 1.2 Bankruptcies and Foreclosures

A Chapter 7 bankruptcy requires 4 years from the discharge date before an
applicant is eligible, absent documented extenuating circumstances (2 years
with extenuating circumstances). A foreclosure requires 7 years from the
completion date (3 years with extenuating circumstances and additional
restrictions). Any bankruptcy or foreclosure in the applicant's history
should be flagged as a condition requiring underwriter documentation review,
even outside the exclusion window.

### 1.3 Late Payments and Collections

More than two 30-day late payments in the trailing 12 months on any
revolving or installment account is a material adverse factor and should be
rated at least MEDIUM risk. Open collections over $2,000 in aggregate must
be paid off or included in the DTI calculation unless excluded by policy
(e.g. medical collections under $250 individually).

## 2. Income Policy

### 2.1 Employment Stability

A minimum two-year employment history in the same line of work is standard.
Gaps under six months with a documented reason (education, medical leave)
are acceptable. Recent job changes within the same field and at equal or
higher income are acceptable and should not be treated as an adverse factor.

### 2.2 Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio

The standard maximum DTI is 43%. DTI between 43% and 50% requires
compensating factors (e.g. strong reserves, low LTV, high credit score) to
be documented as a condition. DTI above 50% is generally not eligible
without an automated underwriting system approval that explicitly permits
the exception.

### 2.3 Self-Employment Income

Self-employed applicants require two years of personal and business tax
returns. Income is generally averaged over the two most recent years; a
declining trend of more than 20% year-over-year requires additional
explanation and may not be usable at face value.

### 2.4 Housing Expense Ratio

The front-end (housing expense) ratio should not exceed 28% under standard
guidelines; up to 35% is acceptable with strong compensating factors.

## 3. Asset Policy

### 3.1 Down Payment Sourcing

Down payment funds must be sourced and seasoned for at least 60 days in the
applicant's account, or accompanied by a gift letter and donor
documentation if gifted. Funds that cannot be sourced should not be counted
toward the down payment or reserves.

### 3.2 Reserve Requirements

A minimum of 2 months of PITI (principal, interest, taxes, insurance) in
post-closing liquid reserves is required for primary residences under
standard guidelines. Investment properties and higher-LTV loans may require
up to 6 months; that threshold is outside the scope of this manual's
baseline case but should be flagged for underwriter judgment.

### 3.3 Large Deposits

Any single deposit exceeding 25% of the applicant's verified monthly income
in the two months prior to application is considered a "large deposit" and
requires written sourcing documentation (paystub, gift letter, sale
document). Undocumented large deposits must be excluded from qualifying
assets.

## 4. Collateral Policy

### 4.1 Loan-to-Value (LTV) Ratio

LTV of 80% or below qualifies for standard pricing with no mortgage
insurance requirement under most conventional programs. LTV between 80% and
90% is acceptable with mortgage insurance. LTV between 90% and 97% requires
additional compensating factors (credit score, reserves) and is the maximum
for most standard first-lien conventional products. LTV above 97% is not
eligible under standard guidelines.

### 4.2 Property Condition

The property must be safe, sound, and structurally secure. Appraisals
noting deferred maintenance that affects habitability (roof, foundation,
electrical, plumbing) require repair completion or a holdback escrow prior
to closing. Cosmetic issues alone are not disqualifying.

### 4.3 Appraisal Validity

Appraisals are valid for 120 days from the effective date. An appraisal
value that is more than 10% below the contract price requires either a
renegotiated price, an appraisal appeal, or additional buyer funds to cover
the gap — the loan amount cannot be increased to match the contract price.

## 5. Fair Lending and Compliance

### 5.1 Prohibited Factors

Underwriting decisions must never be based on race, color, religion,
national origin, sex, marital status, age (provided the applicant has the
capacity to contract), disability, or receipt of public assistance income,
consistent with the Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA) and the Fair
Housing Act.

### 5.2 Geographic Proxies

Underwriters must not use neighborhood, zip code, or other geographic
descriptors as a proxy for creditworthiness. Property-specific risk (e.g.
condition, marketability) must be assessed on its own factual merits, not
on area-level characteristics.

### 5.3 Escalation

Any case with a compliance flag, a bias flag, or a risk score at or above
the senior-review threshold must be routed to a human underwriter before a
final decision is communicated to the applicant.
